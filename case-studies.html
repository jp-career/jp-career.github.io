1) Fremont Medical Center — Legacy-to-Modern Healthcare IT Transformation

Organization: Fremont Medical Center

Career progression: IT Assistant → Network Administrator → IT Manager → Director of Information Systems

Environment: Multi-facility healthcare organization transitioning from legacy systems and limited connectivity into a more modern operational model

Hospital IT Leadership Infrastructure Modernization Multi-Site Operations Clinical Systems Readiness Executive Partnership

Context

When I joined Fremont Medical Center, the organization was operating with an older dumb-terminal practice management and billing environment, DSL-based links between facilities, and decentralized dial-up email. The overall IT foundation needed modernization to better support operations, communications, resilience, and future clinical systems direction.

My role

I grew with the organization from hands-on technical support into IT leadership, eventually reporting at the executive level. Along the way, I helped define, plan, and implement the transition from a limited legacy environment into a more modern and supportable healthcare IT model.

What I led

  • Helped develop and modernize the network environment across facilities.
  • Implemented core Microsoft infrastructure including Windows Server and Exchange.
  • Introduced backup, firewall, proxy, VPN, and redundancy solutions to improve operational resilience.
  • Supported rollout of a new phone system across facilities.
  • Worked closely on EMR vendor selection and implementation readiness.
  • Bridged technical execution with operational and leadership priorities as responsibilities expanded.

Why this matters

This experience gave me a hospital-side foundation that still shapes how I work today: practical execution, infrastructure awareness, leadership growth, and an understanding that healthcare technology only succeeds when it supports real operational and clinical environments.

2) LATAM Clinical Systems Delivery Playbook

Company: Accuray (LATAM)

Products: CyberKnife • Radixact • TomoTherapy • TomoReNew

Scope: Participated in all installs across a multi-country LATAM footprint from 2012–2023

Regional Delivery Leadership Regulated Healthcare Distributor Coordination Go-Live Readiness Forecast Discipline

Context

Successful delivery in LATAM required alignment across distributors, construction readiness, regulatory constraints, logistics, technical teams, and customer stakeholders. Predictability mattered not just for installation, but also for go-live planning, warranty transition, and customer confidence.

My role

I supported execution from pre-sales through installation and go-live, while helping ensure readiness for warranty and service handoff. My work required close coordination across internal teams, distributors, and customer executives, technical leads, and clinical stakeholders.

What I did

  • Standardized execution and handoffs across the project lifecycle to reduce rework and improve delivery quality.
  • Used Salesforce for milestone visibility and Smartsheet for detailed execution from pre-install through go-live.
  • Helped control readiness and quality through governed checklists archived at project close-out.
  • Coordinated across installation, physics, engineering, sales, operations, QA, distributors, and customer teams.

Results

  • Improved install efficiency and reduced avoidable parts replacement through better execution discipline.
  • Helped tighten forecasting variance from 12–24 months down to six months or less, with remaining movement driven mainly by construction and permitting realities.

3) Radixact La Paz Reliability Turnaround

Company: Accuray

Location: La Paz, Bolivia

Product: Radixact

Escalation Leadership Reliability Outcomes Cross-Functional Alignment Customer Trust

Context

A high-altitude deployment introduced non-standard technical requirements, increasing risk around long-term stability, uptime, and customer confidence. The challenge was not just to solve a technical problem, but to protect the overall success of the site.

My role

I helped lead coordination through a complex technical adaptation, balancing timeline pressure with the more important long-term outcome: a stable, supportable clinical system.

What I did

  • Aligned installation, physics, engineering, QA, and operations around the altitude-driven requirements gap and path forward.
  • Supported development and deployment of a high-altitude hardware adaptation for the site.
  • Managed distributor execution and customer communication through go-live preparation.

Result

  • The system became one of the most reliable in the region, with rare downtime and most issues manageable through preventive maintenance rather than repeated crisis response.

4) Unity Connectivity Handover System

Context

Clinical Operations needed site-specific technical and environment data to complete milestones, prepare training, and execute go-lives. That information often lived across multiple owners, chats, documents, and escalation paths.

My role

Designed and implemented a more structured handover approach as an individual contributor, aligning Systems Specialist inputs with Clinical Ops needs and real milestone timing.

Problem

  • Site connectivity data was fragmented across people and channels.
  • Clinical Ops had to repeatedly chase Systems Specialists for the same information.
  • Training readiness and milestone completion were delayed by missing or unclear inputs.

Solution

  • Built a Teams-based global dataset to centralize site-level connectivity and environment information.
  • Standardized fields and a handover checklist so required inputs were complete and more consistent.
  • Clarified ownership and timing so information was available earlier in the project lifecycle.

Impact

  • Reduced repeat outreach and eliminated much of the time spent hunting for information across channels.
  • Improved predictability for milestone readiness, onsite clinical training planning, and go-live preparation.
  • Adopted across 10+ sites since January 2026, with further expansion as more sites moved through trial and readiness stages.
