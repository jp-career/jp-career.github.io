1) Fremont Medical Center — Legacy-to-Modern Healthcare IT Transformation

Context

When I joined Fremont Medical Center, the environment reflected an older era of healthcare IT: a legacy dumb-terminal practice management and billing setup, DSL connectivity between facilities, and decentralized dial-up email. The organization needed not just technical upgrades, but a practical modernization path that would support operations across multiple facilities and enable future growth.

My role

I joined as an IT Assistant, progressed to Network Administrator, then IT Manager, and ultimately Director of Information Systems. Over time, I moved from hands-on technical execution into broader responsibility for infrastructure, systems planning, vendor coordination, and operational support aligned with leadership priorities.

What I led

  • Developed and modernized the organization’s network foundation across facilities.
  • Implemented Microsoft-based infrastructure, including Windows Server and Exchange.
  • Introduced more mature backup, firewall, proxy, VPN, and redundancy capabilities.
  • Supported implementation of an updated phone system across locations.
  • Worked closely on EMR selection and implementation planning.
  • Aligned technical improvements with business, operational, and leadership needs.

Why this case matters

This case anchors my portfolio because it demonstrates more than technical support. It shows my ability to operate inside a healthcare organization, understand how IT decisions affect day-to-day operations, and grow into broader leadership responsibility while helping move a legacy environment into a more modern, reliable model.

Strategic value: This case shows hospital-side credibility, modernization leadership, and a career path built on both execution and increasing scope.

2) LATAM Delivery Playbook — Standardizing Clinical Systems Execution Across the Region

Context

Delivering complex healthcare technology across LATAM required coordination across distributors, customer leadership, clinical stakeholders, regulatory considerations, construction variability, and cross-border logistics. Success depended on making execution more consistent from pre-sales through installation, clinical readiness, go-live, and transition into service.

My role

I participated in all regional installs across a multi-country LATAM footprint from 2012 to 2023 and helped drive end-to-end execution from pre-sales through go-live. My role required alignment across internal teams, distributors, and customer stakeholders across technical, operational, executive, and clinical functions.

What I did

  • Standardized execution and handoff practices across the delivery lifecycle.
  • Used Salesforce for milestone visibility and Smartsheet for detailed execution management.
  • Controlled readiness and closeout quality through governed checklist processes.
  • Coordinated among installation, physics, engineering, operations, quality, distributor teams, and customer stakeholders.
  • Improved discipline around readiness, communication, and project follow-through.

Results

  • Improved install efficiency and overall project quality.
  • Reduced avoidable parts replacement by strengthening execution quality.
  • Improved forecast variance from roughly 12 to 24 months down to 6 months or less, with remaining variability often tied to customer-side construction realities.
Strategic value: This case demonstrates regional leadership, delivery discipline, regulated-environment execution, and the ability to align diverse stakeholders across countries.

3) Radixact La Paz Reliability Turnaround — Leading Through a High-Risk Technical Challenge

Context

A Radixact deployment in La Paz, Bolivia introduced unusual environmental complexity due to high altitude. This created technical risk that could have affected long-term reliability, uptime, and customer confidence.

My role

I helped lead coordination through the escalation, balancing technical realities, project timing, and the long-term success criteria that mattered most: stable operation, dependable uptime, and confidence in clinical use.

What I did

  • Aligned installation, engineering, physics, quality, and operations around the site’s non-standard requirements.
  • Supported development and deployment of a high-altitude hardware adaptation plan.
  • Managed communications and execution alignment with distributor and customer stakeholders through go-live preparation.
  • Kept focus on long-term reliability, not just short-term project completion.

Result

The site became one of the most reliable in the region, with rare downtime and a service profile that was largely manageable through preventive maintenance.

Strategic value: This case shows escalation leadership, calm decision-making in technically difficult conditions, and the ability to help turn a high-risk project into a stable operational result.

4) Unity Connectivity Handover System — Building a Practical Workflow That Scales

Context

Clinical Operations needed site-specific system configuration details, hospital IT information, and readiness inputs to complete milestones, plan training, and support successful go-lives. The problem was not lack of information; it was that the information lived across different people, threads, and documents, creating repeated follow-up and wasted time.

My role

As an individual contributor, I designed and implemented a practical handover approach that aligned Systems Specialists inputs with Clinical Ops needs and gave teams a clearer readiness structure.

What I built

  • A Teams-based global dataset to centralize the clinical operations connectivity data set for each site.
  • Standardized fields and handover expectations so information arrived more complete and consistent.
  • Clearer ownership and timing to make data available ahead of milestones, training, and go-live.
  • A more practical workflow that reduced repeated back-and-forth and made readiness easier to assess.

Impact

  • Reduced repeated outreach to Systems Specialists.
  • Eliminated much of the “hunt for information” across scattered channels.
  • Improved predictability for project milestones, onsite clinical training planning, and go-live readiness.
  • Adopted across 10+ sites since January 2026, with expansion continuing as more sites enter trial.
Strategic value: This case shows that I do not only execute projects; I also build lightweight systems that improve how teams work at scale.
